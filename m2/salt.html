Experiment 02

Salt demonstration

Without a salt, two users who choose the same password store the same hash. A salt is a unique random value mixed in before hashing, so identical passwords produce different stored records.

Same password, different salts

Each run draws two independent 128-bit values from crypto.getRandomValues and hashes salt + password with SHA-256.

Random salts

Why salt matters

A salt is stored next to the hash and is not secret. Its job is to make precomputed rainbow tables useless and to hide whether two accounts share a password. A salt does not turn a weak password into a strong one.
